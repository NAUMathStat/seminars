### Long Memory, Extremes, and Structural Change in Environmental Time Series: Assessing Long-term Trends in U.S. Extreme Ozone

**Date:** Tuesday 1/20 at 4:00-4:50

**Speakers:** Jaechoul Lee, Associate Professor, NAU

**Abstract:**  Long memory and structural change are common features of environmental time series and pose significant challenges for inference on trends in extremes. Many environmental extreme series exhibit persistent dependence, while changes in policy, monitoring practices, and instrumentation can introduce abrupt shifts that may be confounded with long-memory autocorrelation. Ignoring either feature can lead to misleading conclusions. This talk presents recent methodological developments for analyzing long-term trends in weekly maximum ground-level ozone concentrations across U.S. counties. A long-memory extreme value model is developed using a copula-based framework to capture persistent dependence in extremes. Changepoints are estimated using a genetic algorithm that explicitly accounts for long-memory autocorrelation, reducing the risk of spurious detections. The results demonstrate that incorporating changepoints substantially alters estimated long-term trends in 27.5% of counties and reveals pronounced spatial heterogeneity in both long-term trends and return levels. Overall, this work highlights the importance of jointly modeling long memory, extremes, and structural change in environmental time series analysis.

### Unite and Conquer: the folding of impartial combinatorial games

**Date:** Tuesday 1/27 at 4:00-4:50

**Speakers:** Nandor Sieben, Professor, NAU

**Abstract:**  In theory the winning strategy of a combinatorial game can be found by a simple process analyzing the digraph of positions. In practice this is often impossible because the game digraph is too large. Folding the game digraph identifies positions that are essentially the same. Folding constructs a quotient game that is easier to analyze since it has fewer positions. The theory allows for the four isomorphism theorems known from Universal Algebra. Clever foldings make it possible to analyze many interesting games like "Totative" and "Sliding Coins". Joint work with Baltushkin and Ernst.
